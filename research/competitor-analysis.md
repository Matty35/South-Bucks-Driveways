## 0. Verified planning ground truth (the gap topics)

These are the facts our content must get right — verified against primary sources, not competitor sites:

1. **The >5m² permeable surfacing rule.** Since the 2008 permitted-development change, hard-surfacing more than 5m² of a front garden requires planning permission *unless* the surface is permeable (gravel, permeable block paving, porous asphalt, resin **bound**) or rainwater drains to a permeable area (lawn/border) within the property. Areas ≤5m² of repair/replacement are exempt. Sources: [GOV.UK guidance on permeable surfacing of front gardens](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/permeable-surfacing-of-front-gardens-guidance/guidance-on-the-permeable-surfacing-of-front-gardens), [Planning Portal — paving your front garden](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/paving-your-front-garden/planning-permission/). Key nuance competitors fumble: resin **bonded** (scatter-coat) is *not* permeable; standard block paving is *not* permeable — permeable block paving is a distinct product.
2. **Chilterns National Landscape (former AONB) material guidance.** The Chilterns Buildings Design Guide and its checklists advise: limit tarmac area, avoid highly coloured concrete paviours, avoid concrete kerbs / fake cobbles / patterned concrete (suburbanising features), don't mix too many surface materials, and consider bound-gravel finishes. Sources: [Chilterns published advice & guidance](https://www.chilterns.org.uk/what-we-do/planning-and-development/published-advice-guidance/), [Design Guide checklists PDF](https://www.chilterns.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CBDGChecklists2010.pdf).
3. **Article 4 directions & conservation areas.** Buckinghamshire Council maintains ~389 Article 4 directions and ~186 conservation areas. Beaconsfield Old Town is a designated conservation area (first designated 1969) with an Article 4 direction area inherited from the former district council ([planning.data.gov.uk entity](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/entity/7010002022), [Beaconsfield Old Town Character Appraisal](https://media.buckinghamshire.gov.uk/documents/Beaconsfield_Old_Town_Character_Appraisal.pdf)); Chalfont St Giles has a conservation area around the village green; Chalfont St Peter has its own. Article 4 directions withdraw permitted-development rights for works affecting external appearance/frontages, so driveway changes that would normally be permitted can require a planning application. Sources: [Buckinghamshire Council Article 4 directions](https://www.buckinghamshire.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/having-a-say-and-reporting-issues/article-4-directions/), [conservation areas in Buckinghamshire](https://www.buckinghamshire.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/conservation-heritage-and-archaeology/heritage/conservation-areas-in-buckinghamshire/), [Beaconsfield Design Guidance & Codes (2022)](https://beaconsfieldtowncouncil.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/220817_Beaconsfield-RV.04.1.pdf).
4. **Large-frontage drainage design.** Soakaway sizing, ACO/threshold channel placement, permeable sub-base build-up (dig-out typically 150–250mm; reduced-fines/MOT Type 3 stone for permeable systems vs standard Type 1, which is *not* free-draining), edge restraints, and chalk-vs-clay infiltration in the Chilterns. No authoritative competitor coverage exists (see matrices below).

**Headline finding: across all four SERPs and all four known competitors, not a single ranking page covers topics 2 or 3 for Buckinghamshire, and none covers topic 4 with real engineering depth. Topic 1 is covered properly by only ~3 pages across all queries.**

## 6. Cross-cutting synthesis: what wins these SERPs

### The open ring (unique content nobody has)
1. **Buckinghamshire planning/heritage content is a zero-competition field across all four SERPs.** Zero ranking pages cover Chilterns National Landscape material guidance, the Beaconsfield Old Town or Chalfonts conservation areas, or Article 4 directions for Bucks. The only entities ranking for those subjects are the council, planning.data.gov.uk, and generic planning guides. Recommended build: a planning/permissions hub page ("Do I need planning permission for a driveway in Beaconsfield / the Chalfonts?") citing the council's Article 4 list, the conservation-area appraisals, the Chilterns Buildings Design Guide checklist, and the Beaconsfield Design Code — internally linked from every town and service page. Kola is the only competitor with an Article 4 content template (for Winchester), so speed matters.
2. **Drainage engineering depth is absent everywhere.** A technical "how we build it" section — dig-out depths, MOT Type 3 vs Type 1 (two ranking pages get this wrong or fudge it), soakaway sizing, ACO/threshold channels, edge restraints, chalk-vs-clay infiltration — would out-depth every competitor and directly serves the large-frontage GX/Beaconsfield customer (FGD and Ashley have groundworks capability but publish nothing).
3. **The 5m² rule is table stakes, not a differentiator** — the Beaconsfield top two cover it and two national cost guides cover it. We must state it accurately (including the resin-bonded and standard-block-paving traps, which nobody explains properly) but it won't rank a page alone.
4. **Local pricing transparency is a completely open position.** No local company on any of the four SERPs publishes £/m² figures. A Buckinghamshire pricing page with a per-m² table, size-based examples, build-up line items, and real local projects with real totals (the Vuba pattern, localised) has no direct competition.

### Structural playbook (what the winners do)
- **Town-specific location pages win these SERPs** (7/8 top company results at GX; #1 at Beaconsfield and CSG) — but almost all are thin programmatic templates from non-local firms.
