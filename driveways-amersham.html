Old Amersham and Amersham-on-the-Hill

Old Amersham is the county's showpiece: a medieval market town strung along its broad High Street, timber frames behind Georgian brick fronts, the Misbourne running through the water meadows. Its conservation area is among the most carefully guarded streetscapes in Buckinghamshire, and driveway work here is discreet by definition — courtyards behind street frontages, gravelled yards off the back lanes, period-appropriate surfaces detailed to disappear into the setting. Loud paving doesn't get planning sympathy here, and it wouldn't deserve it.

Amersham-on-the-Hill is a different century's town. It grew around the Metropolitan Railway station from the 1890s and boomed between the wars — Metro-land in the literal sense, its avenues lined with gabled 1920s and 1930s family houses, generous semis and detacheds with the classic hill-town frontage: a rising drive, a garage set back, 50–80 m² of surface doing daily family work. This is where most of our Amersham installs happen: resin bound for seamless slopes, block paving in muted tones for the period-minded, and repairs to driveways the decades have tired out.

The hill helps: chalk drainage

Unlike the clay of the Chalfonts, the higher ground here is predominantly free-draining chalk — the kindest ground we work on. Permeable surfaces over an open-graded sub-base usually soak their own catchment without a soakaway, which trims both cost and disruption. The valley floor in Old Amersham is a different conversation, held with the Misbourne in mind. Either way, the drainage logic is in our SuDS guide.

Conservation notes for Amersham

Old Amersham's conservation area covers the High Street and its hinterland, and Article 4 provisions tighten control over works that would normally be permitted development — frontage and boundary changes included. On the Hill, the protections are lighter but not absent: designated pockets, tree preservation orders along the avenues, and the Chilterns National Landscape design guidance shaping what planners want to see when consent is needed. The national surfacing rule applies throughout: over 5 m² of front garden, permeable or drained within the boundary — rarely a constraint in practice, since the surfaces that suit Amersham best are permeable anyway.

Street-by-street detail is in our conservation areas guide; the rules themselves are in the planning permission guide. We confirm your address's position on the design visit, in writing.

The villages on the Amersham round

From Amersham our work spreads naturally along the valley and over the ridges: Little Chalfont eastward along the Metropolitan line, Chalfont St Giles down the A413 where the Misbourne continues toward the other Chalfonts, and Penn and Knotty Green over the hill toward Beaconsfield. Village work leans to the traditional — gravel approaches, bound aggregate in golden flint tones, clay setts at gateways — the palette the Chilterns guidance favours and the one that looks inevitable against brick-and-flint walling.

Amersham questions we hear most

What works for a courtyard behind an Old Amersham frontage?

Self-binding gravel, bound aggregate in a golden tone, or reclaimed-style clay setts — surfaces that read as though they've always been there. Old Town courtyards are usually small, so the premium for doing them beautifully is modest, and the conservation-area setting rewards it. We detail falls carefully here: the valley floor sits close to the Misbourne's water table.

My drive on the Hill slopes to the garage — which surface copes?

Resin bound, almost every time. It's seamless, grips well with the anti-slip dressing, and holds a gradient that would send gravel migrating downhill within a season. We pair it with a channel drain across the garage threshold so the slope's run-off is caught at the bottom — the detail missing from most ponding garage forecourts we're asked to fix.

Do I really not need a soakaway on the Hill?

Usually not — the chalk under most of Amersham-on-the-Hill drinks faster than a driveway can supply it, so a permeable surface over an open-graded sub-base is its own drainage. We still check: made ground, filled hollows and clay caps exist locally, and a ten-minute test hole on the design visit is cheaper than a wrong assumption.
