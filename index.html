Driveways for conservation areas

Much of South Buckinghamshire sits inside the Chilterns National Landscape or a designated conservation area — Beaconsfield Old Town, the village greens of the Chalfonts, the lanes around Penn and Jordans. Several streets also carry Article 4 directions, which remove the usual permitted-development rights and mean changes to a frontage can need planning permission that wouldn't be required elsewhere.

Most driveway companies never mention any of this. For us it's part of the job: we check your street's designations before we design anything, recommend materials that sit naturally against brick, flint and render — bound aggregate finishes, muted block tones, traditional gravel — and steer you away from the bright paviours and patterned concrete the Chilterns design guidance warns against. Our guide to driveways in conservation areas and the Chilterns explains the designations town by town.

Questions we're asked most

Do I need planning permission for a new driveway?

Usually not — if the surface is permeable. Front-garden surfacing over 5 m² must either be permeable (resin bound, permeable block paving, gravel) or drain to a lawn or border, otherwise planning permission is required. In conservation areas and on streets with Article 4 directions the position can differ, which is why we check your street's designations as part of the free design visit.

Where does the rainwater go?

Through the surface, by design. Our resin bound and permeable paving systems are laid over a free-draining sub-base so rain soaks away rather than running onto the highway. On larger frontages we size soakaways to your ground conditions — chalk and clay behave very differently in this area — and fit channel drains at thresholds and garage doors.

Which surface suits a period home best?

It depends on the frontage. Bound aggregate and gravel finishes are the quiet, traditional choices the Chilterns design guidance favours; muted clay blocks in traditional patterns also sit well against brick-and-flint. We bring samples to the design visit so you can judge them against your own elevation.

How long does an installation take?

Most domestic driveways take between three days and a week depending on size, dig-out depth and drainage work. We confirm a schedule in the quotation and keep the site tidy, contained and safe throughout.

What does the 10-year guarantee cover?

Workmanship across the whole build-up — the sub-base, drainage and surface we install. It's written into your quotation, not a verbal promise, and sits alongside the manufacturer warranties on the materials themselves.
